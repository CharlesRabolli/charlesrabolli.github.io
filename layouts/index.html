1 Circulation · 2024 first author

Where the mark sits on an mRNA matters

Nanopore direct RNA sequencing mapped METTL3-dependent m6A sites in adult cardiomyocytes. Whether the mark fell in the coding sequence or the 3′ UTR tracked with differences in protein output, and it pointed to targets in mitochondrial metabolism.

read the paper →

2 JCI Insight · 2025 first author

The heart helps set whole-body metabolism

On a Western diet, deleting METTL3 in cardiomyocytes protected mice from the diet's harmful effects, while raising it worsened weight gain, adiposity, and glucose intolerance. Cardiac m6A stabilizes FGF1 mRNA, a heart-to-fat signal.

read the paper →

3 RNA · 2025 first author

An m6A reader drives cardiac growth under stress

Mice lacking YTHDF3 in cardiomyocytes showed less pathological remodeling after pressure overload. YTHDF3 controls stress-induced protein synthesis and cardiomyocyte size, which makes it a candidate cardioprotective target.
